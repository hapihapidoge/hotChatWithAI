## Answer

One of the main problems is which AI you use online or offline, free or paid, which model and which model version, what level of knowledge it has and which data sources it is based on, the context, temperature for more or less creativity and so on. If you ask the same question to different AIs, they can give different answers. Sometimes even several answers can sound very plausible, although one, several, or even all of them may be wrong. The problem can be roughly represented like this: AI A - Answer A AI B - Answer B AI C - Answer C Then you do not automatically know which answer is actually correct. 1. Always questioning is one possibility, but you do not have to completely check every single answer Are you sure? How do you know that? Where does this information come from and do you have evidence? Which parts of your answer are uncertain or could be wrong? What speaks against your own answer? This forces the AI to check its explanation again. 2. Use a second or several sources/AIs for important facts If two or more AIs say the same thing, it is an indication but not proof, because they can all make the same mistake. 3. Check an independent source for important things Especially for science, law, medicine, finance, etc. 4.
