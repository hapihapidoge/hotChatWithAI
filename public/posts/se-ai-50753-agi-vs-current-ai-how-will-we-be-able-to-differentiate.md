## Question

I dont know if this is the right site to ask it but suppose we create a AGI , how would be able to differentiate from current LLMs?I mean what tests would a AGI pass that current LLMs cannot?

## Answer

My sense is that the main differentiator would be not so much one "intelligence test," but rather the ability to reliably show that the system can outperform current LLMs on things they currently perform poorly on. For example, I'd look at: Can long-horizon planning breakdown and achieve a complex task over many steps without a human in the loop? Continual learning: Are there mechanisms in the model for it to take in new experiences, learn from them and use that experience in the future without retraining? Transfer/generalization: Can it truly not know the situation, with a new setting completely unlike what it trained on? Autonomous reasoning: Does it realize when it is on the wrong track, adapt its plan and still be able to reach its goal? Self-motivated goals: Could it generate and follow through with goals of its own that are not directly reflected in the prompt? I would also design the tests to be more flexible and interactive. A real AGI should be able to consistently handle problems it has never seen before in different areas of knowledge, changing its approach according to the trial-and-error it receives. That is much more significant than a higher score on current benchmarks. The challenge is in defining a test that doesn't end up being yet another goodie to be written for and optimized for by subsequent systems.
